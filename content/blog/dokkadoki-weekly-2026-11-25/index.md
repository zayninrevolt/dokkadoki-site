If a long row of numbered spines makes starting manga feel like homework, try something with a smaller commitment. This week's pick is **Tatsuki Fujimoto's Look Back**, a single book about two young people brought together by drawing comics. No catch-up list required.

## A short book about making things

Fujino is confident; Kyomoto is withdrawn. Their shared love of drawing brings the two small-town girls together. That is the starting point in [VIZ's official synopsis](https://www.viz.com/manga-books/manga/look-back/product/7195), and enough of the story to know before opening it.

The English paperback is **144 pages**, with a **Teen Plus** rating. It is a backlist recommendation, not a new manga release. Fujimoto also created *Chainsaw Man*, but you do not need to read that series first: this is a separate story with its own characters.

The appeal of choosing a short manga is practical. You can try an unfamiliar creator without deciding whether to collect a shelf of their work. Here, the premise also gives you something to consider beyond plot: what happens when making art becomes a friendship, rather than something you do alone?

For a first read, resist the urge to race through just because the book is short. Notice where a page lets the pictures carry the moment, and where you find yourself slowing down. Afterwards, think about whether the drawing or the dialogue stayed with you. That is a more useful starting point for choosing your next manga than worrying about which famous series you ought to have finished.

VIZ offers a free preview on the linked page. Try that before buying, and check your chosen bookseller for current UK price and availability.

## From paper to the cinema

There is a timely reason to revisit the title. **Hirokazu Koreeda's film adaptation of Look Back reaches UK and Irish cinemas from 22 January 2027**, in Japanese with English subtitles. [Anime Limited's announcement](https://blog.alltheanime.com/look-back-theatrical/) was published on 18 November. Local screenings will need checking before you plan a trip; the national release date is not a Manchester booking.

For a lighter viewing prospect, **CITY the Animation** follows university students Nagumo, Niikura and Izumi through increasingly strange everyday situations. Kyoto Animation adapts the manga by **Keiichi Arawi**, creator of *Nichijou*. Anime Limited schedules its Collector's Edition Blu-ray for **14 December**, including a 72-page booklet, art cards and an A3 poster. [Release details](https://blog.alltheanime.com/city-the-animation/) and [the official shop](https://www.alltheanime.com/collections/city-the-animation) are useful starting points for comparing editions.
