A London culture-and-sport talk on 17 August explores Japan's professional keirin circuit. *The Guardian*'s Japan and Korea correspondent Justin McCurry joins Reynolds Technology director Jim McCafferty to look beyond the Olympic sprint and explore the industry, rituals and social history around the sport.

## From post-war rebuilding to a national racing culture

Keirin was introduced in Japan in 1948 to raise money for rebuilding infrastructure after the Second World War. The Japan Society describes a year-round professional circuit of about 2,400 riders, culminating in December's Keirin Grand Prix. Gambling is central to the domestic sport: it is one of only four sports on which betting is legal in Japan, with races run at indoor and outdoor velodromes around the country.

A pacemaker leads nine riders before leaving with roughly a lap and a half remaining, opening a sprint in which competitors can approach 70kph. Keirin joined the Olympic track programme in 2000, but this discussion focuses on the larger Japanese culture behind that international event.

## Speakers and context

McCurry is the author of *War On Wheels: Inside Keirin and Japan's Cycling Subculture*, published in English in 2021 and Japanese in 2023. McCafferty joined bicycle-tubing manufacturer Reynolds Technology as a director in 2024 after a career in Asia-Pacific equity research. Their conversation connects elite racing with gambling, bicycle engineering and wider cultural, social and economic dynamics in Japan.
