Three Durham University researchers connect twentieth-century Japanese history with the ways people encounter the past today. The evening begins with Rachel Barclay introducing the Oriental Museum's Japanese collections before moving through politics, musicology, colonial history and performance.

## Three case studies

Dr Adam Bronson examines polls and petitions as interactive media shaping public opinion from the 1880s to the 1920s. Dr Amanda Hsieh explores compositional art competition amid global fascism and the colonial complexities of twentieth-century East Asia.

Dr Fusako Innami looks at dance and spatial notation, tracing gestures, movement and marks across East Asia and Europe during the 1930s.

The published programme is a research talk rather than a workshop, performance or exhibition opening. No guest signing, merchandise, gaming or food offer is listed.
