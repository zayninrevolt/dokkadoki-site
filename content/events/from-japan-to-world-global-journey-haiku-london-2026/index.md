Professor, poet and biographer Robert Crawford follows haiku from its Japanese roots to its place as one of the world's most widely recognised poetic forms. The lecture draws on his 2026 Yale University Press book *Haiku* and the lives of people who helped the form travel internationally.

## A global literary journey

Crawford's story includes a working-class London bookseller, a Japanese professor, a Mexican diplomat and an exiled African American writer. It also considers why haiku continues to appeal across generations and continents, including in conversations about climate change, social media and AI.

Crawford is Emeritus Wardlaw Professor of Poetry at the University of St Andrews. His work includes nine poetry collections and biographies of Robert Burns and T. S. Eliot.
