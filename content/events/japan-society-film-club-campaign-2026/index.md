The Japan Society’s recurring online Film Club turns to Kazuhiro Soda’s *Campaign* on 2 September. The first-Wednesday club asks participants to watch the selected film independently, then meet on Zoom for an informal conversation about Japanese cinema, culture and society.

## An election campaign observed from the inside

Released in 2007, *Campaign* follows Kazuhiko Yamaguchi, a stamp-and-coin shop owner selected by Japan’s Liberal Democratic Party to contest a local by-election. He has no political experience or established connection to Miyamae Ward, yet is drawn into a tightly choreographed street campaign of marching, bowing, chanting and constant supervision by party operatives.

The documentary uses that unlikely candidacy to examine the rituals and pressures of Japanese grassroots politics. Rather than treating the meeting as a conventional screening, the club expects participants to arrive having already seen the film and ready to discuss its political and social observations.

## Kazuhiro Soda’s observational approach

Soda is known for a minimalist observational method that avoids heavy narration, formal interviews and visible directorial intervention. *Campaign* established that approach internationally; his later documentaries include *Mental* (2008), *Peace* (2010) and *Inland Sea* (2018). The Film Club presents the work as both a political documentary and an entry point into Soda’s wider approach to everyday life in Japan.
