A shipbuilding employee with ideas for saving the sea receives an unexpected marriage proposal from a mermaid princess. That is the starting point for *ChaO*, which brings an unusual romantic comedy to UK and Irish cinemas from **Friday 4 December**.

Directed by **Yasuhiro Aoki** and made by **STUDIO 4°C**, the film follows Stephan and Chao in a future where humans and mermaids have a troubled relationship. The [official synopsis](https://chaofilm.co.uk/synopsis) promises giant robots and shopping trips alongside the romance. An anxious engineer trying to navigate all of that is a rather different prospect from the usual meet-cute.

Anime Limited's [release announcement](https://www.otakunews.com/Article/12203/chao-uk-and-ireland-cinema-release-details) confirms screenings in **Japanese with English subtitles** and notes the film's Jury Award at the 2025 Annecy International Animation Film Festival.

The premise makes this worth keeping on the cinema watchlist, especially for anyone interested in animation beyond adaptations of familiar manga. This is a preview recommendation, rather than a review: the attraction is the strange pairing and the world built around it.

The [official UK film site](https://chaofilm.co.uk/) currently offers ticket notifications. Check there for participating cinemas and bookings before planning a trip; a national release date does not guarantee a screening at your nearest venue.

## Manga for anyone who worries about the party's provisions

Fantasy adventures tend to involve plenty of walking and fighting. Ryoko Kui's **Delicious in Dungeon** starts with a more immediate concern: how everyone is going to eat.

After a disastrous encounter with a dragon, Laios and his companions need to return to the dungeon to rescue a missing party member. They have lost their money and provisions, so Laios proposes eating the monsters along the way.

That is the setup in [Yen Press's volume-one synopsis](https://yenpress.com/titles/9780316471855-delicious-in-dungeon-vol-1), and it is a good reason to try the opening book if you like fantasy with a practical, slightly alarming problem to solve.

There is also a reassuringly definite reading commitment. Yen Press confirms that the [complete manga runs to 14 volumes](https://yenpress.com/titles/9798855413786-delicious-in-dungeon-the-complete-box-set). Start with one before committing to the full feast. This is a backlist recommendation, not a new-release announcement.

## On the gaming radar

**Shade of the Marionette: The Story of Piyorita** is worth a look for *WitchSpring* followers interested in action combat. KIWIWALKS' [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5034390/Shade_of_the_Marionette_The_Story_of_Piyorita/) describes a story-led action RPG with weapon and spell crafting, traps, and defeated bosses that can become controllable dolls.

The combination gives prospective players something more specific to investigate than a trailer's spectacle. Steam currently labels the release **Coming soon**, so treat it as a game to follow rather than a dated purchase plan.

## Dates worth a look

- **23 January 2027, Manchester: SiM at Club Academy.** A further-ahead local booking alert, with **The Haunt** listed as support. Live Nation gives doors as **7pm** and requires under-16s to attend with an adult aged 18 or over. Its page labels tickets General Onsale; check the seller for current prices, fees and availability. [Official details and booking](https://www.livenation.co.uk/event/sim-manchester-tickets-edp1674586).

- **23 November, London: Hisaishi conducts Hisaishi, Royal Albert Hall, 7pm.** The programme centres on concert music, including the UK premiere of Hisaishi's *Concerto for Orchestra*, rather than a Ghibli soundtrack selection. The RPO currently marks **few tickets left** and advertises tickets from **£69**, including a £4 transaction fee charged once per order. [Programme and booking](https://www.rpo.co.uk/tickets-memberships/event/2026/hisaishi-conducts-hisaishi).

- **14 December, London: matsuri 26 at Outernet Live.** **Hitsujibungaku, Kyary Pamyu Pamyu and Otoboke Beaver** share the bill, making this a wider-UK pick for readers interested in several sides of Japanese music. Doors are **7pm**; entry is **14+**, with adult accompaniment for ages 14–16 and physical photo ID required. Check the seller for current prices and availability. [Official details and booking](https://www.livenation.co.uk/event/matsuri-26-london-tickets-edp1710326).

These are independent recommendations, not events hosted or organised by DokkaDoki.
